About

Studying muscle health through medical imaging.

I am a Ph.D. student at the Biomedical Image Intelligence Lab, Nara Institute of Science and Technology (NAIST). I analyze large-scale medical imaging datasets to segment skeletal muscle on CT and quantify its volume, density, and related imaging biomarkers. My research explores federated learning and source-free domain adaptation to help models work across hospitals, scanners, and imaging protocols.

Before starting my Ph.D., I studied Mathematics at the University of Waterloo, joined the Department of Medical Informatics, Faculty of Medicine, Osaka University as a research student, completed my master’s degree at NAIST, and took part in an exchange at Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT), Germany, where I worked with a computer graphics group.

Outside the lab, I enjoy dancing, photography, bouldering, tennis, travel, and exploring local food.

Research

Research interests

Large-scale musculoskeletal CT analysis, federated learning, and source-free domain adaptation for medical imaging across institutions.

quantitative anatomy

Musculoskeletal Analysis

I develop automated pipelines to segment skeletal muscle and surrounding adipose tissue in large CT datasets. The goal is to quantify muscle volume, density, and composition for studies of musculoskeletal health and changes over time.

privacy-aware AI

Federated Learning

I investigate how hospitals can train shared segmentation models while keeping patient images at each site. I study how differences in scanners, acquisition protocols, and patient populations affect performance across institutions.

cross-site generalization

Source-Free Domain Adaptation

I explore how to adapt a pretrained segmentation model to unlabeled CT scans from a new hospital without access to the original training data. My focus is on denoising pseudo-labels generated by the model so that incorrect predictions have less influence during adaptation.

Projects

Projects across medical imaging, clinical AI, and computer graphics.

Biomedical Image Intelligence Lab, NAIST

Federated Learning for Quantitative Musculoskeletal CT Analysis

My current Ph.D. research focuses on privacy-preserving medical image analysis for quantitative musculoskeletal CT assessment. I work on segmentation-based pipelines for extracting clinically meaningful imaging biomarkers, such as muscle volume, density, and quality, while exploring federated learning as a way to improve cross-institutional generalization without directly sharing patient data.

The project also explores whether source-free domain adaptation can improve muscle segmentation on scans from a new site when the original training data are unavailable.

Computer Graphics Group, KIT · Nov. 2025–Mar. 2026

Spatiotemporal Denoising for Monte Carlo Ray Tracing

During my exchange at Karlsruhe Institute of Technology, I worked with a computer graphics group on image denoising for Monte Carlo ray tracing. The project focused on improving the visual quality of noisy rendered images by using spatial and temporal information to reduce sampling noise while preserving important scene details.

This experience broadened my interest in image formation, denoising, geometric representations, and the connection between computer graphics and medical image analysis.

Faculty of Medicine, Osaka University · 2022–2024

ECG-Based Deep Learning for Postoperative Cardiovascular Risk Assessment

This early-stage medical AI project used ECG data for cardiovascular risk assessment. The broader goal was to develop deep learning models for prognosis prediction after cardiovascular surgery, including recurrence and mortality risk.

The initial step was to build the training pipeline and conduct preliminary experiments on age regression and sex classification from ECG signals. These proxy tasks served as a starting point for learning meaningful ECG representations before moving toward more complex clinical outcomes.

Publications

2026 · Scientific Reports

Machine learning-based mortality prediction for pediatric fulminant myocarditis using cytokine profiles

Sarah Jing and collaborators.

This study analyzes blood-derived cytokine profiles together with demographic and clinical information to classify mortality and survival groups in pediatric fulminant myocarditis. The model uses cytokine and patient-level features to support risk prediction, then examines important features that may help explain which immune signals contribute most strongly to the classification.
